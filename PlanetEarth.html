Planet Earth

Our world is packed with amazing ecosystems, record-breaking landmarks, and fascinating science. Here is a quick guide using lists!

Steps to Help Our Planet:

  1. Unplug chargers and electronics when you aren't using them.
  2. Bring cloth bags instead of plastic ones when shopping.
  3. Recycle paper, glass, and metal cans properly.
  4. Take shorter showers to save fresh water.
  5. Share what you learn about nature with your friends.

Amazing Earth Landmarks:

  • Mariana Trench — The deepest part of the ocean, plunging nearly 11,000 metres down.
  • Nile River — One of the longest rivers in the world, stretching over 6,600 kilometres.
  • Antarctica — The coldest, windiest, and driest continent on Earth.
  • Grand Canyon — A massive gorge in the US carved by the Colorado River over millions of years.

Key Terms:

Ecosystem

A community of living things interacting with their physical environment.

Atmosphere

The blanket of gases surrounding Earth that gives us air to breathe and blocks harmful sun rays.

Sustainability

Using natural resources carefully so they don't run out for future generations.

How to Recycle Properly (Steps):

  1. Rinse out empty food jars, cans, and plastic bottles before binning them.
  2. Flatten cardboard boxes so they take up less space in the recycling bin.
  3. Keep wet food scraps and greasy pizza boxes out of the paper recycling.
  4. Separate batteries and old electronics to drop off at special e-waste centres.
  5. Check the recycling symbols on packaging before tossing anything away!

Incredible Wildlife Records:

  • Blue Whale — The largest animal ever known to have lived on Earth, reaching lengths of up to 30 metres.
  • Peregrine Falcon — The fastest bird, capable of diving at speeds over 320 km/h.
  • Giant Sequoia Trees — Some of the oldest living organisms, with lifespans exceeding 3,000 years.
  • Monarch Butterfly Migration — These butterflies travel thousands of kilometres from North America to Mexico each year.

Frequently Asked Questions:

  • Why is the sky blue? — Earth's atmosphere scatters blue sunlight in all directions much more than other colors because blue light travels in shorter, smaller waves.
  • Why do we have seasons? — Seasons happen because Earth is tilted on its axis as it orbits the Sun, giving different parts of the planet more direct sunlight at different times of the year.
  • How much of Earth's water is drinkable? — Less than 1% of all water on Earth is accessible fresh water, while the rest is salty ocean water or locked away in glaciers and ice caps.
  • How fast is Earth moving through space? — Earth spins on its axis at about 1,600 kilometres per hour while orbiting the Sun at an incredible 107,000 kilometres per hour.
